STEP 9 -- POLYGON PLANARITY, AND A RENDERING GAP (2026-07-21)
========================================================================

    Arose from the question "is the stored corner order a rendering
    optimisation?" -- asked against a proposal to canonicalise polygons
    by rotating each to its lexicographically least starting corner.

    THE PROPOSAL WAS WRONG. Measured over the 176 retail models:

        polygons                       122535
          triangles                    101669
          quads                         19959
          5..15 corners                   907
        non-planar beyond 0.1% of own extent
                                         3478   (16.7% of n-gons)

    For a planar polygon the starting corner is free: any fan pivot
    describes the same surface. For a NON-PLANAR one it is not --
    fanning from corner 0 versus corner 1 gives different triangles and
    a different surface. So the starting corner is load-bearing data for
    one polygon in six. Rotating it would have changed the model and
    made genuinely different shapes compare equal.

    => --geom preserving corner order is CORRECT, not accidental, and
       the "unclosed degree of freedom" I claimed is not one.

    Caveat on the measurement: the plane was fitted through the first
    three corners, so polygons whose first three are near-collinear read
    high (the 107% worst case in bomber09 is almost certainly that). A
    best-fit plane would give a cleaner number, not a different verdict.

STEP 10 -- WHY SIX DIGITS AND NINE, PRECISELY (2026-07-21)
========================================================================

/home/lnicoara/build/mine.d/fs2.d/pofer/dump.cc:59  digits_t
    compared { "(%.6g %.6g %.6g)", "(%.6g %.6g)" }   -- the oracle format
    exact    { "(%.9g %.9g %.9g)", "(%.9g %.9g)" }   -- the mesh dump

    Six is not "roughly float precision" and nine is not padding. They
    are the two standard constants for IEEE binary32, and they answer
    OPPOSITE questions:

        FLT_DIG          6 = floor(23 * log10 2)
            any 6-digit DECIMAL survives decimal -> float -> decimal
            unchanged. For data that STARTS as decimal: how much can be
            safely put into a float.

        FLT_DECIMAL_DIG  9 = ceil(1 + 24 * log10 2)
            every distinct FLOAT prints distinctly and reads back
            bit-identical. For data that STARTS as float: how much is
            needed to reconstruct it.

    printf's %g defaults to 6 significant digits, which is why 6 feels
    like "the" float precision -- it is printf's default and it happens
    to equal FLT_DIG. It is the wrong one of the two for a dump that
    gets read back, because POF coordinates start as floats.

--- measured, on the real coordinates

    Sampled distinct float32 coordinates from 40 models' mesh dumps:

        distinct values                    151845
        fail to round-trip through %.6g    145611   (95.9%)
        fail to round-trip through %.9g         0

    Not edge cases. Nearly every coordinate in these models. Six digits
    suffice to RECOGNISE a coordinate and not to RECONSTRUCT it.

--- consequence, already seen

    capital01 carries submodel offsets to 1066, where six significant
    figures resolve to about 0.01. Round the offset and the vertex
    separately, then add them downstream, and 1271 of 4347 corners land
    somewhere the model does not put them (step 5). At nine digits, 0.

    So --geom at 6 is fit for its actual job -- deciding whether two
    readers found the same polygon -- and unfit for anything read back.
    --mesh at 9 is lossless.

    Worth admitting: the right precision for --mesh was arrived at by
    discovering a bug, not by deciding. The six in --geom is inherited
    from what fs2/tests/pof_dump.cpp happened to print, and is the one
    remaining place where an implementation's default, rather than a
    decision about what model equality means, defines the comparison.

STEP 11 -- ORDERING: WHERE CANONICALISATION BELONGS (2026-07-21)
========================================================================

    All of this came out of one question: "suppose retail parses in the
    same order as pofer -- would the dump be identical?"

--- MEASURED: yes. The sort absorbs exactly one reader.

    Scratch POF_NOSORT switch added to BOTH dumpers (reverted after):
      /home/lnicoara/build/mine.d/fs2.d/fs2/tests/pof_dump.cpp:485
      /home/lnicoara/build/mine.d/fs2.d/pofer/dump.cc:139

    Unsorted, all 176 models, 128693 lines:  0 diff lines.

    HONEST CAVEAT: that is not a property of the format. pofer was
    deliberately given the retail DUMPER's walk when it was written. What
    it establishes is that the sort currently absorbs exactly one
    reader's divergence -- libpof's front,back,pre,post,on.

    Invocation note: fs2's dumper goes through cfile and takes a GAME
    ROOT, not a path:  pof_dump --geom rundir [model.pof ...]

--- RETAIL HAS NO SINGLE TRAVERSAL ORDER

/home/lnicoara/build/mine.d/fs2.d/fs2/code/model/modelinterp.cpp:741
    model_interp_sortnorm:
        if (g3_check_normal_facing(...))  back, on, front   // facing
        else                              front, on, back

    The RENDER walk flips per node, per frame, by which side of the split
    plane the eye is on. That is the painter's algorithm -- the reason a
    sort-by-normal tree exists at all in a renderer with no depth buffer.
    model_collide_sortnorm uses a fixed order; pof_dump mirrors THAT one.

    So traversal order is not a property of the model, nor even of a
    reader: it is a property of a reader's PURPOSE, and one of retail's
    own orders changes as the camera moves.

    => "mandate a traversal order in the spec" is now REJECTED. Retail
       could not satisfy it, and it would force every reader to carry a
       dump-specific walk distinct from its working one. The sort is
       order-agnostic and survives readers whose order legitimately
       varies. I proposed mandating it and was wrong.

--- THE DEFECT: the canonical order is defined by the SERIALISATION

    Both dumpers sort the FORMATTED STRING, so the order is a function of
    the print format. Same polygons, different sequence:

        cargo03        71 polys   --geom vs --mesh order: differs at 20
        fighter2s-03  580 polys   differs at 210
        capital01    2239 polys   differs at 0
        t-laser        12 polys   same
        support02     362 polys   differs at 10

    4 of 5 sampled models. Purely because six- and nine-digit strings
    collate differently. It also couples the two dumpers through their
    printf strings: they must FORMAT identically in order to SORT
    identically.

--- CANDIDATE ANSWER (the user's)

    Leave the parsers alone; each keeps whatever walk suits it. Define,
    for the sole purpose of dumping, a total order on a submodel's
    polygons computed FROM THE DATA:

      - it is a VIEW (vector of indices/pointers) sorted by a comparator
        that reads values. The model is const. NOTHING is mutated,
        reordered or rewritten within a record.
      - the key is the COMPLETE RECORD: every corner's position, normal
        and uv, in stored corner order, compared as values not text.
      - ties are free: records equal on a full key are indistinguishable.
        A PARTIAL key (first corner, centroid, hash) breaks this -- ties
        become material and resolve by the view's construction order,
        i.e. the traversal order we are quotienting away. Fails silently,
        and only on models with coincident faces.
      - SUBMODELS ARE NOT REORDERED. Their index is referenced: parent,
        LOD list, debris list, eye sobj_number, turret physical_parent.
        File (OBJ2 chunk) order is already reader-independent.

    Buys: order invariant under precision/syntax/field set; --geom
    becomes a relational PROJECTION of --mesh (same rows, fewer columns);
    migration verifiable because a precision change reorders nothing;
    dumpers decouple (agree on a key in a spec, not a printf string).

--- WHEN CANONICALISING IS LEGITIMATE AT ALL

/home/lnicoara/build/mine.d/fs2.d/pofer/dump.cc:57  zero()
    Both dumpers fold -0 to +0 on every printed value. That IS the
    serialiser altering a value. Decided: it is fine, and the reason is
    categorical rather than a matter of degree:

      -0.0 and +0.0 DENOTE THE SAME NUMBER (IEEE: they compare equal).
      Choosing +0 picks a representative of a REAL equivalence class.
      That is what canonicalisation means.

      0.692 and 1.0 denote DIFFERENT numbers. libpof asserted an
      equivalence that does not hold. That is data loss.

    RULE: canonicalising within a real equivalence class is legitimate;
    inventing an equivalence is not.

    Open caveat: "real" is scoped to how the data is used. +-0 differ
    under 1/x, atan2, copysign and byte comparison; none arise for POF
    geometry. Whether scoping an equivalence to current use is sound
    practice or a landmine is RFC question 6.

    239 lines. Written for an outside expert. Commits 6bb3f04 (initial)
    and 8491212 (this material folded in). Linked from pofer's README.

    Six questions; the live ones are (1) is the candidate answer the
    right shape, (2) is there a principled way to derive what may be
    quotiented -- we learned both answers by MEASURING, (4) how to

--- OPEN DECISION, NOT TAKEN: pin libpof against the oracle

    NOT "make libpof the oracle" -- my earlier phrasing was sloppy and

    corpus (sha256 fe696cb0...) whose provenance is retail's loader;
